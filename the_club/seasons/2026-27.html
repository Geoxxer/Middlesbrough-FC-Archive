Season Overview

The 2025/26 season was a dramatic campaign for Middlesbrough, marked by managerial upheaval, a sustained promotion challenge and an extraordinary run to the Championship Play-Off Final.

Boro began the season under new head coach Rob Edwards and quickly established themselves among the Championship's leading sides. Middlesbrough spent much of the opening months around the automatic promotion places, and by November they were firmly in the promotion race.

Edwards' time at the Riverside came to an abrupt and controversial end in November. Premier League side Wolverhampton Wanderers approached Middlesbrough over their former player, who had only joined Boro in June. The initial approach was rejected, but Edwards subsequently made clear that he wanted to speak to Wolves. He was stood down from the Birmingham City match, with Adi Viveash and Harry Watling taking charge, while Edwards did not conduct the team's final training session or his scheduled pre-match press conference.

Middlesbrough eventually agreed to allow Edwards to speak to Wolves and a compensation deal was reached, but his departure caused considerable anger among Boro supporters. Edwards left Middlesbrough with the club sitting second in the Championship, only five months after signing a three-year contract, to take charge of Wolves.

Adi Viveash took temporary charge before Kim Hellberg was appointed as Edwards' successor. Under Hellberg, Middlesbrough continued their promotion challenge and produced an excellent run of form through the winter. A series of victories saw Boro climb back into the automatic promotion places, including an important 2–1 comeback victory over Stoke City at the bet365 Stadium.

The run reached its peak on 9 February, when Boro travelled to Bramall Lane to face Sheffield United. A 2–1 victory moved Middlesbrough to the top of the Championship, completing a remarkable turnaround after the disruption of the managerial change.

However, their time at the summit was short-lived. In the following match, Middlesbrough travelled to Coventry City in a meeting between the Championship's top two. Coventry won 3–1, with Haji Wright scoring a hat-trick, and returned to the top of the table. The defeat marked the beginning of a difficult spell for Boro, who subsequently dropped points against Oxford United and Leicester City and struggled to reproduce the consistency of their earlier winning run.

Despite the downturn in form, Middlesbrough remained in contention for automatic promotion for much of the closing stages of the season. Ultimately, the points dropped during the latter part of the campaign proved costly, and Boro finished fifth in the Championship to secure a place in the Play-Offs.

Middlesbrough's Play-Off campaign became one of the most unusual episodes in the club's history. Boro faced Southampton in the semi-finals, with the tie initially ending in a 2–1 aggregate defeat after a 0–0 draw at the Riverside and a 2–1 defeat at St Mary's. Southampton were subsequently expelled from the Play-Offs following a disciplinary ruling concerning the unauthorised filming of Middlesbrough's training sessions, allowing Boro to advance to the final.

At Wembley on 23 May 2026, Middlesbrough faced Hull City with promotion to the Premier League at stake. Boro held Hull to a goalless draw for almost the entire match, but Oli McBurnie scored in the fifth minute of stoppage time to give Hull a 1–0 victory. Middlesbrough therefore ended the 2025/26 season just short of promotion, after a campaign that had seen them reach the top of the Championship, fall out of the automatic promotion places and ultimately experience an extraordinary Play-Off journey.
